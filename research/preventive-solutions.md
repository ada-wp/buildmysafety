# Build My Safety — practical preparation and solution framework

Research checked September 23, 2026. US-first starting coverage. This is a product-design draft using primary public guidance and official provider descriptions. The proposed templates, routing and user outcomes are our product ideas; they have not yet been professionally reviewed or tested with users. Links checked does not mean a particular local service is available or suitable.

**The philosophy determines the solutions**

The original project describes safety as agency, capability and a life that can support you. Its practical ingredients are knowledge, people, resources, systems and practiced actions. The resulting confidence is “I can handle it.” Original homepage (historical local reference), capability statement (historical local reference).

A useful solution can make something understandable, available, accessible, agreed or practiced. It need not involve buying something or finding a friend. Preparatory actions can reduce difficulty when something happens; they do not necessarily prevent the event. For example, a food reserve can make an unwell day easier without preventing illness.

The six concern groups below come from our earlier research. They describe user problems and are not six replacement product areas. Map each card into My Backup, If I'm Out of Action, My Home or My Body; use transitions as context.

**The solution-building method**

| Route | What we help the user do | Product output |
|---|---|---|
| Learn | Understand one relevant skill, system or choice | Brief explanation and a credible learning source |
| Prepare | Put useful supplies or information within reach | Personalized inventory and a small missing-items list |
| Set up | Configure an existing tool, reminder or routine | Device/provider-specific setup guide |
| Arrange help | Establish a suitable person, service or support route | Search criteria, comparison questions and an outreach script |
| Practice | Try the arrangement in an ordinary, low-risk way | A completion check and a small rehearsal |
| Maintain | Revisit something when it expires or circumstances change | User-chosen reminder or review trigger |

These are options for our content library, not six compulsory tasks for every user. Choose the lightest route that resolves the person's current gap.

**1. Illness or injury without practical help**

| Proposed solution | Prepare now | What our product supplies | Evidence / resource and access limits | Done when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A small sick-day shelf | Set aside familiar, easy-to-use shelf-stable foods suited to dietary needs, drinking water and personally useful supplies; plan how to replenish them | Inventory first, then a short missing-items list and an optional shopping/reminder note | [CDC personal needs](https://www.cdc.gov/prepare-your-health/take-action/personal-needs.html). Supply costs vary. Storage, allergies and accessibility affect choices. | Useful items are reachable; one missing essential has an affordable acquisition plan |
| A care-access and essentials pouch | Gather a charger, key contact information and personally necessary items; note where care/insurance information is kept | Editable pouch checklist plus a care-provider questions sheet | [CDC individual kit guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/disability-emergency-preparedness/people-with-disabilities/build-a-kit.html). Ask a pharmacist/clinician about medication supply and storage. Keep personal records private. | The pouch can be located and the person knows the next ordinary step to reach their care provider |
| A pet-care handoff | Record the pet's routine and vet contact, identify a suitable backup, and discuss access before it is needed | Pet-care card; specific request message; provider comparison questions | [Red Cross pet preparation](https://www.redcross.org/get-help/how-to-prepare-for-emergencies/pet-disaster-preparedness.html); [Rover](https://www.rover.com/) for paid local search. Confirm fit, rates, availability and access arrangements. | A person/provider has actually agreed to a defined role; the handoff is understandable |

Other useful candidates from the existing 60-area map: reachable recovery space, non-emergency transport fallback, an absence handoff, and asking a procedure team what support is required beforehand. Transport booking is not medical triage or proof that a service can provide post-procedure assistance.

**2. Carrying every household responsibility**

| Proposed solution | Prepare now | What our product supplies | Evidence / resource and access limits | Done when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A low-energy day plan | Identify the few tasks that genuinely need attention and simplify one recurring task | A short “essential / can wait / ask for help” template; optional task breakdown | [Goblin Tools](https://goblin.tools/About) offers task-support tools; existing web functions are free. Use it for ordinary planning, not unreviewed medical, electrical or other hazardous instructions. | The person has a short plan they can realistically follow |
| A calendar that distributes help | Agree who could do a specific meal drop-off, ride or household task and when | A request script, role boundaries and a calendar template | [Meal Train](https://www.mealtrain.com/) offers free basic meal coordination; [Lotsa Helping Hands](https://lotsahelpinghands.com/) coordinates meals, rides and tasks. Both need participating helpers; neither creates an available caregiver automatically. | A participant has accepted one task and knows the relevant details |
| A pre-researched household helper | Before a task becomes urgent, compare relevant services and ask about scope, cost and timing | A two-option comparison sheet and inquiry message | [Taskrabbit](https://www.taskrabbit.com/) is a candidate for listed household tasks in supported locations; [FTC contractor checks](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/how-avoid-home-improvement-scam) help with relevant hiring decisions. Specialist work needs appropriately qualified providers. | One option has been checked for the actual task; unanswered questions remain visible |

Creative route: when a person has no nearby contact, a distant friend might help research options or organize a calendar. Confirm the person's willingness and permissions; do not hand over passwords or assume remote help replaces physical care.

**3. Financial fragility on one income**

| Proposed solution | Prepare now | What our product supplies | Evidence / resource and access limits | Done when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A short essentials-and-bills map | Write down the next essential bills, dates and contact routes | One focused worksheet rather than a full financial overhaul | [CFPB Your Money, Your Goals](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/educator-tools/your-money-your-goals/toolkit/). Free US worksheets; legal consequences and benefits vary. | Essential commitments and who to contact are easy to locate |
| One realistic buffer goal | Choose a foreseeable expense and determine whether any money can be set aside without undermining essentials | A modest user-chosen target and review reminder | [CFPB emergency-fund guide](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/an-essential-guide-to-building-an-emergency-fund/). Avoid universal savings targets or automatic transfers that cause overdrafts. | A feasible next step exists, including seeking assistance when saving is not currently possible |
| A support route researched in advance | Find relevant local food, utility, housing or care assistance and check how to apply | Eligibility questions, contact details and a document-to-gather list | [United Way 211](https://www.211.org/). Referral and listing are not approval, guaranteed funding or immediate service. | The person knows how to contact one relevant program and what still requires confirmation |

Creative route: ask an employer, insurer or existing membership whether a relevant benefit is already included; look for lending or gifting options before shopping. These are inquiry prompts, not claims that a particular user is entitled to a service.

**4. Home readiness, access and everyday security**

| Proposed solution | Prepare now | What our product supplies | Evidence / resource and access limits | Done when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A small home-readiness check | Learn the building's arrangements, locate alarm instructions, and check one appropriate maintenance task | A renter/owner-specific task card and a message to the landlord if needed | [US Fire Administration smoke-alarm guidance](https://www.usfa.fema.gov/prevention/home-fires/prepare-for-fire/smoke-alarms/). Follow device/local instructions; qualified electricians handle hardwired installation. Some local fire departments offer alarm assistance. | The check is completed, or the responsible person has received a clear request |
| An interruption kit | Gather useful light/charging supplies and tailor food, water and other essentials to the situation and official local guidance | “Already have / need / borrow or obtain” inventory; small first action | [Red Cross kit guidance](https://www.redcross.org/get-help/how-to-prepare-for-emergencies/survival-kit-supplies.html). An initial small step is not a complete emergency kit. | Selected items work, are accessible, and remaining gaps are recorded |
| An access-and-repair plan | Learn the building's lockout and maintenance process, save verified contacts, and prepare questions before hiring | A home contact card and appointment-verification script | [FTC contractor guidance](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/how-avoid-home-improvement-scam). Verify relevant licensing, insurance, written terms and the actual provider. | The person knows the appropriate contact route and has a usable appointment/hiring checklist |

Creative route: local fire-department assistance, a building manager's orientation, or a library's local referral/lending service may help more than a shopping link. Check local availability. Do not recommend security modifications that obstruct exits or bypass building requirements.

**5. Meaningful connection and someone who notices**

| Proposed solution | Prepare now | What our product supplies | Evidence / resource and access limits | Done when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A role-based support map | Distinguish company, remote coordination, local practical help and paid/community services | A small editable map and one specific request | [Lotsa Helping Hands](https://lotsahelpinghands.com/) can coordinate willing helpers. A contact's existence is not consent or availability. | One realistic role is agreed, or a missing role is accurately identified |
| A mutually agreed check-in | Choose an appropriate purpose, frequency, privacy preference and what missed contact means | A discussion script and a setup guide for a suitable existing tool | [Snug plans](https://snug.helpscoutdocs.com/article/61-snug-pricing) and [Apple Check In](https://support.apple.com/guide/personal-safety/use-check-in-for-messages-ips56b5bc469/web) are different options. | Familiarity through a low-pressure shared activity | Find a recurring local activity or exchange that fits the person's interests and limits | Local search prompts, one invitation script and a participation choice | [Buy Nothing](https://buynothingproject.org/about) supports local gifting of goods/services. Creative route: a bounded reciprocal arrangement, such as an occasional errand exchange, can be easier to discuss than asking someone to be an all-purpose emergency contact. Do not assume reciprocity is required to deserve help.

**How the food and “urgent package” example could work**

Use separate purposes rather than a universal shopping list:

- A sick-day shelf supports ordinary daily needs when energy is low.
- A small personal pouch keeps selected essentials and contact information easy to locate.
- A home/evacuation kit follows the person's local hazards, household needs and authoritative preparation guidance.

Our proposed card might say: “Make one low-energy day easier: check what food and essentials you already have, choose one gap you can afford to address, and put those items somewhere accessible.” That is an entry action, not a complete preparedness certification. Dietary requirements, food storage, available space, mobility, pets and budget should shape the result. Supply and medication specifics require appropriate sources and professional input where relevant. [CDC personal needs](https://www.cdc.gov/prepare-your-health/take-action/personal-needs.html), [CDC individual kit guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/disability-emergency-preparedness/people-with-disabilities/build-a-kit.html).

The product can also suggest a small learning action, such as finding suitable [Red Cross first-aid training](https://www.redcross.org/take-a-class/first-aid/first-aid-training/first-aid-classes). Course format, price, accessibility and hands-on practice need checking; reading our summary is not equivalent to training.

**What to show in each personalized recommendation**

1. What is already useful or in place, when supported by the answers.
2. One relevant thing to strengthen, without claiming a risk score.
3. Why this particular step fits the person's situation.
4. One first action with up to three instructions; estimated effort and cost.
5. At most a few relevant links, each saying what it helps with and how to use it.
6. A usable template, script or checklist when that removes work.
7. A lower-cost, lower-energy or no-helper route where feasible; otherwise state the unresolved need.
8. A concrete completion check and optional maintenance trigger.

For the first result, show the main action and one best resource. Keep alternatives expandable. Do not expose the entire research library at once.

**Research and recommendation standard**

Store a solution ID, concern IDs, product area, context, prerequisites, action, alternatives, completion check, source URLs, jurisdiction, known costs, last-checked date and content-review status. Distinguish “link checked,” “provider says,” “local availability confirmed” and “arrangement agreed.” Only the last two can establish a real local option or commitment.

Public guidance supports the underlying preparation ideas; it does not validate our questionnaire, routing or AI. Use reviewed content and verified resource IDs. AI can explain and sequence those materials, but should not invent providers, prices, diagnoses, eligibility or instructions for hazardous tasks. Generic task-breakdown tools should not generate the safety-critical substance.

US sites in this document are starting references. Elsewhere, use the relevant national/local emergency agency, fire authority, health system and assistance directory. Do not carry US numbers, entitlements or service coverage into another country.

Access note: direct retrieval of Ready.gov kit/food, the English FEMA app page, USDA shelf-stable-food guidance and Findhelp's public search homepage was blocked during this check. They are not represented here as fully opened and verified recommendations. Accessible CDC, Red Cross, USFA and 211 sources support the starting content instead. The local library/community-center ideas still require location-specific research.

**A first set worth testing**

Start with a sick-day shelf, a personal essentials pouch, a pet handoff, a home contact/readiness card and one support/assistance route. These test preparation, knowledge, tools and people without requiring every user to have a ready-made network. Choose one per participant based on fit. Test whether the guidance saves work, results in something usable, respects constraints and leaves the person knowing what to do next.
