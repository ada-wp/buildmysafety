## Proposed structure

**Category → situation or concern → selectable tasks.** This is content organization within our established topic → concerns → suggestions flow, not another mandatory screen.

| Working category, not final copy | Example situations to investigate |
| --- | --- |
| Sick days & care | Finding care without an established doctor; getting essentials while resting; covering pet care or other responsibilities. |
| Home & repairs | Knowing the route for repairs; choosing appropriate help; preparing for interruptions; managing access to the home. |
| Practical help & connections | Finding occasional help without nearby friends; getting to know useful local services; establishing mutually agreed support. |
| Going out & travel | Planning a workable return journey; preparing for loss of phone access; arranging home or pet care while away. |

These are four candidate groupings, not a commitment to launch four categories. Keep money out of navigation. Moving and first-time solo living are context tags. Feeling more capable is a purpose across the app; it does not require a separate generic self-help category. Existing interests can lead into these groups without adding dozens of top-level categories.

An action may fit several situations but should have one canonical outcome and content record. For example, arranging a pet sitter can be relevant to illness or travel; crossing categories must not create duplicate tasks or completion credit.

## Example: preparing to hire help for a home repair

This situation could contain several genuinely different options, offered when relevant:

- **Learn how repairs are handled where you live.** For a renter, help locate the building's reporting route and clarify whom to contact outside office hours. Do not assume the user must hire or pay a contractor.
- **Learn how to check a repair professional.** Supply the appropriate official starting route and explain what to check for the relevant location and trade. The outcome is knowing how to evaluate a future candidate, not collecting an unverified phone number.
- **Compare estimates for a real upcoming job.** Explain scope, exclusions and differences in proposed work so the user can make an informed comparison. This belongs when work is actually planned, not as a requirement to solicit quotes for an imaginary repair.
- **Agree how a planned visit will work.** Clarify the requested work and how additional work will be approved before the visit. Tailor practical arrangements to circumstances the user has shared.

The [FTC's consumer guidance](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/how-avoid-home-improvement-scam), checked October 12, supports checking licensing with the relevant state or county, requesting proof of insurance, comparing written estimates and specifying work in writing. Local requirements vary. Do not inherit the reference article's universal cost, deposit, licensing or trust-score claims. These examples are directions for future task writing, not a reviewed home-repair pool.

## How this improves the pool and AI writing

For each situation, ask what the person may not know exists, what makes an option usable, and what she could put in place before needing it. Supply a starting point and the decisive questions; avoid assigning vague research or reorganizing known information.

Use the existing six angles—Understand, Discover, Prepare, Arrange, Practice, Make it easier next time—as writing aids, not required categories or a quota. Retain compact task details and a meaningful finish. A prospective service is not confirmed support.

Keep the agreed category package: roughly thirty distinct crafted tasks, a simple optional roadmap, checked resources and a tailored prompt. Store shared content once. Ordinary requests use fitting pool tasks; AI helps with a meaningful personal adaptation or a new supported need. Give it relevant reviewed knowledge rather than asking it to reproduce a blog or invent local facts. At most one task in a batch may contain links, with up to two resources in that task.
