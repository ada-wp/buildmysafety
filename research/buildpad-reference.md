## How to use it

- Consult its situation and source leads when evaluating future categories and task gaps. Read the relevant original source before relying on a claim or publishing a recommendation.
- Keep population, country, date and evidence type attached. A qualitative concern can inspire a solution without proving its prevalence or rank.
- The report supports further investigation of sick-day preparation, usable practical support, home access/privacy and home-management knowledge. These are research directions, not validated demand for our specific app.
- Do not infer that every solo-living woman is lonely, lacks support, wants monitoring or needs a financial category. Money remains outside navigation; affordability can constrain any task.
- Do not transplant the report's emergency-detection framing into our positioning. Preparation, agency and enjoying solo life remain the purpose. A contact, a key and a check-in app are not interchangeable with reliable help.
- Existing source leads do not authorize collecting home access details, medical histories or legal documents in our app. Researching a service is not proof of access or suitability.

## Suggested follow-up research

These are proposed briefs for Ada to run in Buildpad, not research already completed. Prioritize Brief 1. If trial time allows, run Brief 2, then Brief 3. Avoid another broad pain-point list.

### Brief 1 — Specific situations, overlooked options and useful actions

We are building My Safety, a mobile web app for women living alone. It helps people build agency and a sense of safety through practical preparation before something goes wrong. The experience is: choose a topic, select relevant concerns, receive up to three useful tasks, choose what to save, then complete manageable steps. Our first sick-day task draft exists. We need deeper research for future category and solution-pool decisions, not app design or a generic safety checklist.

Start with home and repairs. Investigate concrete situations such as finding trustworthy help, arranging a service visit, knowing who handles a home problem, dealing with an unresponsive property manager, preventing a lockout, and preparing for an outage. Let evidence change this shortlist. Prioritize US women living alone; separate renter and homeowner needs, first-time and experienced solo living, local support and no nearby support. Do not invent demographics where sources do not disclose them.

For each well-supported situation, report: the problem in the person's own words; what they tried; the overlooked knowledge, service or arrangement that could help; one concrete advance action; what usable outcome it produces; who it does and does not suit; and the one circumstance that most changes the recommendation. Propose a plain-language checkbox only when choosing it would meaningfully change task selection.

Use community discussions to discover needs and reputable primary sources to check solutions. Include source URLs, dates, population/context and confidence. Visible votes are an attention signal, not proof of correctness. For external services, verify geography, likely costs or pricing uncertainty, first-use requirements and limitations. Do not promise availability, trustworthiness or universal renter rights.

Aim for 15–20 distinct, evidence-backed opportunities, not thirty padded task titles. Include negative cases where the app would not help. We will craft the final task copy ourselves. Avoid weapons, hazardous DIY, fear-based tricks, repeated list-making and assumptions that users already have a doctor, helpful neighbour or money to spend.

### Brief 2 — What makes practical preparation useful enough to return to?

Research actual user behavior around preparedness, home-management, practical life-planning and small-action apps. Our product helps women living alone complete useful preparation, with a gentle animal companion. It is not emergency monitoring or therapy. We need to understand whether people want a one-time setup, occasional help when circumstances change, or continued use.

Find the closest relevant products and direct user accounts, including negative reviews, abandoned tools and free substitutes. Distinguish direct competitors from engagement analogies such as Finch. For each, identify what users actually value, what brings them back, what causes abandonment, where suggested actions feel trivial, and any credible evidence of payment. Separate company claims from independent evidence; do not infer retention or willingness to pay from downloads, star ratings or having a subscription price.

Look for triggers such as moving, an illness experience, a new pet, a service problem or a change in available support. Do not presume daily use is desirable. Recommend three inexpensive pilot tests and the user behavior each would measure. Include evidence that could disprove the product hypothesis. Give links, dates, source context and uncertainties. Do not invent conversion or retention figures.

### Brief 3 — Where to meet people already seeking this help

Research low-maintenance ways to reach US women living alone who are actively looking for practical preparation or support. Focus on exact searches, recurring community questions and useful content opportunities rather than broad lifestyle reach.

For 8–10 specific opportunities, provide: the real user question or search phrase; a source URL and date; the channel/community; what answer is missing; one useful free content idea; and the matching in-app topic or action. Check community self-promotion rules before suggesting participation. Include search, relevant newsletters or small communities where supported by evidence. Distinguish urgent help-seeking from advance preparation; do not route emergencies into an app funnel.

Prioritize calm, concrete assistance that reveals useful options. Avoid crime sensationalism, demographic targeting based on inferred distress, and promises of protection. Separate observed demand from assumed keyword volume or audience size. Finish with the three smallest experiments a solo founder could run, what to measure, and what a weak result would mean. Do not contact or post to anyone.
