Safety Project V0 Build Spec
Implementation guide for the free Safety Scan and paid Safety Journey

Version 1.0 | 18 September 2026

Build a small, dependable product that helps someone notice a practical gap, choose one manageable action, and build everyday support over time.

The free product is a 14-question Safety Scan, an immediate personalized result, and an optional emailed Safety Map. The paid product is Build My Backup System, a four-week digital Safety Journey for $29 USD, paid once.

The four areas are My Backup, If I’m Out of Action, My Home, and My Body. Life transitions provide context. They are never a fifth area, a risk multiplier, or a reason to pressure someone to buy.

This specification is the implementation contract for the founder and Codex. It supplies customer copy, deterministic rules, integration contracts, delivery steps, operational safeguards, and acceptance tests. Build the free journey first; activate checkout only after the paid materials and fulfillment have passed testing.

Release principle
Personalization selects from a fixed, human-reviewed library. No model generates, rewrites, ranks, or delivers safety advice at runtime. Scores describe reported preparation, not a person’s actual safety, health, or likelihood of harm.

Decision owners
The founder approves product wording, operational policies, vendors, and pricing. A suitable human reviewer checks each action for scope, accessibility, and foreseeable harm before publication. Codex implements and tests the approved version. Draft copy in this document must not be represented as already professionally reviewed.

Launch defaults
English-language pilot for adults aged 18 and over; women are the initial audience, while the language welcomes anyone who finds the planning useful. No diagnosis, emergency response, safeguarding assessment, insurance advice, or professional certification is offered.

Stage 1  Build the free experience locally
Implement the copy, typed content library, scoring function, four-area results, and test fixtures using fake data. Prove that the same answers always produce the same output. No vendor credentials are needed for this stage.

Stage 2  Connect a staging environment
Create a separate Tally test form, Airtable test base, Make scenarios, and Brevo test templates. Deploy a protected preview. Connect the full scan-to-map flow and test failure recovery before sharing publicly.

Stage 3  Release the free pilot
Complete human content review, privacy notice, support address, domain authentication, accessibility checks, deletion test, and live smoke test. Invite a small group. Keep the paid CTA hidden until Stage 4 passes.

Stage 4  Activate the paid offer
Prepare all four workbooks and emails before charging anyone. Connect Stripe in test mode, verify fulfillment and refunds, then switch to live credentials and approved checkout policies.

Stage 5  Decide with evidence
Review activation, action completion, willingness to pay, refunds, and support effort. Improve the scan or content before adding software features.

Done means
A real person can complete the scan, receive the correct result, carry out one appropriate action, request a private email map, unsubscribe, and request deletion. An operator can recover a failed run without guessing or accidentally sending every message again.

02 Positioning and product boundaries
Safety as practical preparation

Positioning
For women who want everyday life to feel more manageable, Safety Project helps turn vague worries into small, practical preparations across support, continuity, home, and personal care navigation. The benefit is greater readiness and agency. The emotional ambition is “I can handle it,” never a guarantee that nothing bad will happen.

Philosophy
Safety can be strengthened through knowledge, people, resources, systems, and practiced actions. A useful next step should be specific, affordable where possible, and within the person’s control. A lack of money, reliable contacts, mobility, or time must not be framed as personal failure.

Four areas
My Backup: people and services someone could realistically ask for practical help.
If I’m Out of Action: continuity for everyday responsibilities if someone is unavailable.
My Home: access to trusted household guidance, maintenance contacts, and simple preparation.
My Body: organizing questions and knowing how to reach qualified care; no symptom assessment or treatment.

Transition context
Moving, changes in household or relationships, caregiving, work changes, and changes in health or energy can make old arrangements less useful. The scan may add one neutral reminder to revisit arrangements. It must never infer abuse, pregnancy, disability, diagnosis, income, or relationship status beyond what the user explicitly selects.

Deliverables for the free user
Four descriptive area summaries; up to three reported strengths; up to three things to strengthen; one first action; a private printable result page; optionally an email containing the same map. There is no paywall to see results and no email requirement to complete the scan.

Out of scope for V0
Accounts, passwords, dashboard, native app, community, chat, AI recommendations, live monitoring, location tracking, emergency alerts, contact vaults, medical records, document uploads, appointment booking, automatic messages to family, subscriptions, and personalized professional advice. Do not add an overall safety score or red danger meter.

08 Deterministic scoring specification
Internal preparation scores only

Answer values
READY = 2; PARTLY = 1; NOT_YET = 0; UNSURE = 0; NA and SKIP = excluded. Keep the original answer enum so that UNSURE gets different language from NOT_YET. Score only Q01–Q12.

Area calculation
For each three-question area, n is the number of non-excluded answers and sum is their point total. If n = 0, score is null. Otherwise score = sum / (2 × n). Keep numerator and denominator for comparisons; do not compare rounded percentages.
If n < 2: status = NOT_ENOUGH_INFORMATION regardless of the numerical score.
If n ≥ 2 and score ≥ 0.75: status = REPORTED_READY.
If n ≥ 2 and 0.40 ≤ score < 0.75: status = PARTLY_IN_PLACE.
If n ≥ 2 and score < 0.40: status = START_SMALL.
No overall score. Do not display numbers or percentile rankings to customers.

Evidence lists
Already solid contains the approved strength statement for each READY answer, in Q01–Q12 order, maximum three. The full map still shows all four area summaries. If none are READY, use: “You’ve made time to look at your arrangements. We don’t have a specific ready item to highlight from these answers.” Do not invent a strength.
Worth strengthening contains up to three non-ready, non-excluded items. Order the chosen first-action question first, then other items by question ID. For UNSURE use “You weren’t sure whether [topic] is in place.” For PARTLY use “You’ve started [topic]; checking the remaining detail may help.” For NOT_YET use “You haven’t yet put [topic] in place.” If none, use: “Your answers did not identify a specific item to strengthen. Skipped items have not been assessed.”

Selecting an area
Eligible = n ≥ 2 AND at least one answer is PARTLY, NOT_YET, or UNSURE. Choose the eligible area with the lowest score. If tied, use Q14 if it names a tied area. Otherwise use BACKUP, CONTINUITY, HOME, BODY in that order. This ordering is a product convention, not a clinical or risk priority.

Selecting an action
Within the chosen area choose the earliest question ID among NOT_YET or UNSURE answers. If none, choose the earliest PARTLY answer. Use its fixed action ID. Never add generative text. Store quiz_version, rules_version, content_version and selected action ID with every map.

09 Routing exceptions and worked examples
No missing-data penalties

Not enough information
If no area is eligible and at least one area has n < 2, show “There isn’t enough information to choose an area for you.” Let the user choose one of the four areas on the result page. Do not rescore excluded answers. Selection uses the area’s default action: B01, O01, H01, or M01. If the user does not choose, offer G01, the private reflection action. Mark route_reason = insufficient_information.

All applicable arrangements ready
If no area is eligible and every area has n ≥ 2, all applicable answers must be READY. Select G02, a maintenance reminder. Copy: “You reported usable arrangements across the areas you answered. Pick one to revisit when circumstances change.” Never call this a clean bill of health.

Personal preference after the result
Offer “Choose a different first step” under the primary action. This opens four area buttons, then the three actions in that area. The user may select one. Keep the original route for evaluation and record selected_action_id separately. No new questionnaire is needed. Manual choice must not claim to be a recommendation based on missing data.

Transition sentence
For MOVE, HOUSEHOLD, CARE, WORK, ENERGY, or OTHER, add only: “When life changes, old arrangements may need another look. Choose a step that fits your situation now.” For NONE or SKIP add nothing. Do not repeat the selected transition in emails or analytics.

Fixtures for automated tests
F01: all READY → four REPORTED_READY areas; G02; no strengthening items.
F02: all NOT_YET, no preference → BACKUP, B01; four START_SMALL areas.
F03: all NOT_YET, Q14 HOME → HOME, H01.
F04: BACKUP [READY, PARTLY, READY], others READY → BACKUP score 5/6, REPORTED_READY but still eligible; B02. Area readiness must not hide a remaining gap.
F05: BACKUP [READY, SKIP, NA], others READY → no eligible area; insufficient-information route and G01 until choice.
F06: all SKIP → four NOT_ENOUGH_INFORMATION summaries; G01; no gap claims.
F07: BACKUP [PARTLY, UNSURE, READY], others READY → B02, not B01; unstarted/uncertain takes precedence over partly started.
F08: change Q13 only → scores, selected area and action unchanged.
F09: one unknown enum or missing Q06 → reject ingestion; do not produce a map.

12 Action library for people and continuity
Approved wording candidates for human review

B01  Identify one possible source of help | 5 minutes
Privately write down one person or service you might ask for a specific practical task if you were unwell for three days. Name the task, such as collecting groceries. You do not need to contact anyone today.
Done: one possible source and one task are written down privately.
Fallback: if nobody comes to mind, identify an official local community information service to explore later. It is acceptable to leave this unfinished. Do not store names in Safety Project.

B02  Check one realistic offer of help | 5–10 minutes
If it feels appropriate, ask your chosen person or service: “If I needed help with [task] for a few days, is that something you could sometimes help with?” Note any limits privately. An offer is not guaranteed availability.
Done: you know one type of help they can or cannot offer.
Fallback: draft the question without sending it. Do not contact someone if doing so feels unsafe or uncomfortable.

B03  Find one alternative | 5 minutes
Privately list one different person or service you could explore if your first option were unavailable. Note how you would find their current contact details. Do not assume a service is available or free until you check.
Done: one alternative is listed with a way to check availability.
Fallback: bookmark an official local directory; no outreach is required.

O01  Write a three-item responsibility list | 5 minutes
Privately list three everyday tasks that would need attention if you were unavailable for three days. Choose the one that would be hardest to postpone. Do not include passwords or sensitive personal records.
Done: three tasks and one priority are written down.
Fallback: write just one task if three feels too much.

O02  Make one basic handover note | 10 minutes
Choose one routine task. Write plain instructions someone appropriate could follow. Keep passwords, financial access details, and sensitive health information out of the note. Decide privately where to keep it and who, if anyone, should know it exists.
Done: one usable instruction note has a deliberate storage location.
Fallback: create the note for yourself without sharing it.

O03  Explore a backup for one task | 10 minutes
For one responsibility that cannot wait, identify a person or service you could ask about a backup arrangement. Check suitability, consent, availability, and cost before relying on it. Care arrangements may need additional professional or local checks.
Done: you have identified what must be checked before the arrangement can be used.
Fallback: write down the unresolved question; do not improvise care or access arrangements.

13 Action library for home and care navigation
No technical repairs or medical decisions

H01  Save one trusted guidance source | 5 minutes
Find your local government or emergency management website and bookmark its household preparation guidance. Check that it covers your location. You do not need to disclose your location to Safety Project.
Done: one relevant official source is saved where you can find it.
Fallback: write “find official local guidance” on your private list. This is not an action for an active emergency.

H02  Identify a repair contact route | 5 minutes
Find the appropriate way to report a household repair or concern: your landlord, building manager, utility, or a qualified local professional. Save the contact route privately and check when it is monitored.
Done: you know where to report one type of concern.
Fallback: use an official website to identify the correct contact. Do not attempt electrical, gas, structural, or other hazardous work based on this product.

H03  Choose one household check | 5–10 minutes
Read relevant official local guidance or a manufacturer’s instructions. Choose one basic preparation item you can review safely, such as locating the instructions for a household alarm. Follow the source’s directions and seek qualified help if unsure.
Done: you have identified the item and its authoritative instructions.
Fallback: save the instructions for later. Do not climb, dismantle equipment, or perform a test that you cannot safely carry out.

M01  Find a non-urgent care contact route | 5 minutes
Find the official contact or booking information for a qualified healthcare service you could approach with a non-urgent question. Check opening hours and how appointments are requested.
Done: you know a current route for asking a non-urgent care question.
Fallback: use an official local health-service directory. Do not use this step to decide whether a symptom is urgent; seek appropriate qualified or emergency help when needed.

M02  Start a private appointment question list | 5 minutes
In a place you control, write one question you want to ask at a healthcare appointment. You can add what you hope to understand or what practical barrier you want to discuss. Keep the contents out of this scan.
Done: one question is written down privately.
Fallback: write “What should I ask about next?” as a reminder. The product does not interpret or answer medical questions.

M03  Name one access need | 5 minutes
Privately identify one practical thing that could make attending care easier, such as transport, interpretation, accessibility, or time away from another responsibility. Check with the provider or appropriate support service what options actually exist.
Done: one access need and one place to ask about it are identified.
Fallback: prepare the question without making a booking. Do not promise that a service is available, affordable, or suitable.

14 Generic actions and editorial control
Make the library safe to maintain

G01  Choose one thing to make easier | 3 minutes
Privately finish this sentence: “If I were unavailable for a few days, I would like to make ___ easier.” You can choose a related area in your map, or leave the reflection there for now.
Done: one possible focus is written down, or you have decided to return later.
Fallback: no action is required. You can use the scan without sharing more information.

G02  Set one review reminder | 3 minutes
Choose one arrangement you reported as ready and add a private reminder to revisit it in a month or after a relevant life change. Check whether the information and people involved are still available.
Done: one reminder is set in a place you control.
Fallback: note an event that would prompt a review instead of choosing a date.

Content record for every action
id; area; triggering_question_id or generic; title; estimated_minutes; exact_steps; done_when; fallback; boundary; source_ids; content_version; reviewer_name; reviewer_role; reviewed_at; status; next_review_at. Allowed statuses: draft, approved, retired. Production builds reject any referenced action whose status is not approved.

Human review checklist
Check that the action is within practical planning scope, does not imply diagnosis or urgency assessment, has a low-effort alternative, does not assume a trusted household or supportive family, does not ask users to disclose private details, and does not substitute for local professional guidance. Verify official links and avoid universal claims about services or costs.

Review process
Founder prepares the action and its rationale. A reviewer with relevant competence checks home and care-navigation wording; obtain specialist review where wording exceeds general organization. Record approval rather than inventing credentials. Review again after a complaint, a substantive source change, or at six months. Retire unsafe content immediately and pause affected sends.

Runtime rule
The deployed content file is the approved source of truth. Airtable is an operational store, not a live content-editing interface. Editing a rule or action requires a versioned code change, fixture update, review, and deployment. Old maps use their stored snapshot, except where a safety correction requires withdrawal and replacement.

Sources and limits
The actions are product-design proposals, not validated interventions. Official preparedness and healthcare-conversation resources inform review, but do not endorse the product or validate its scoring. Do not use logos or imply endorsement.

19 Architecture and source of truth
One rule engine and a small operational store

Default stack
Codex builds a Next.js App Router site in a private GitHub repository; Vercel hosts it. Tally collects the scan. Next.js verifies submissions and computes the deterministic result. Make queues and coordinates storage and email. Airtable holds minimal operational records. Brevo sends requested maps, consented follow-ups, and paid delivery. Stripe Payment Links are added only for the paid phase.

Flow
Browser starts a signed scan session → Tally receives hidden identifiers → Tally posts a signed webhook to Next.js → Next.js validates and builds the map → Make accepts a normalized job → one sequential intake scenario upserts Airtable records → browser reads its map through a private Next.js endpoint → scheduled Make worker sends Brevo emails and records outcomes.

Why Airtable is the default
The operator can inspect statuses and resolve failed jobs without a dashboard. Use one storage choice, not Airtable plus Google Sheets. Google Sheets is a possible manual-pilot substitute, but it lacks robust uniqueness and concurrency controls; substituting it requires redesigning the lookup, serialization, and retry steps. The executable specification below uses Airtable only.

Responsibility boundaries
Next.js owns field validation, signed sessions, content versions, rules, authorization, and private result responses. Make owns sequential intake, deduplication, delivery job scheduling, and retry coordination. Airtable owns stored state, not business logic. Brevo owns email transport and provider suppression. Stripe owns payment truth.

No durable background work inside an HTTP response
The Next.js webhook responds successfully only after Make acknowledges that the job entered its queue. Do not use an unawaited promise after returning 200. If queue acceptance fails, return an error so Tally can retry. Once accepted, Make’s failed-run recovery handles later errors.

Operational constraints
This design suits a small pilot, not high concurrency. Airtable has no enforced unique constraint. Exactly one sequential intake scenario writes new scan/contact/job records, and exactly one scheduled worker processes outgoing mail. No cloned active scenarios or manual parallel replays. If this becomes a bottleneck, migrate to a transactional database before increasing traffic.

25 API and payload contracts
Enough detail for a deterministic implementation

POST /api/scan-session
Input: adult_confirmed=true and allowlisted source_code. Enforce same-origin/CSRF protection, body limit, and rate limiting. Output: scan_id, session_proof, quiz_version plus signed cookie. Rotate the cookie when a new scan starts; warn that an earlier anonymous map may no longer be available in the same browser.

POST /api/tally
Authenticated by Tally signature. Input: actual Tally event envelope. Output: 202 only after Make queue acceptance; 400 for schema/version errors; 401 for invalid signature/proof; 503 for temporary queue failure. No sensitive payload logging. The adapter produces the normalized payload below.

Normalized scan job example
kind: scan
event_id: vendor event ID
form_id: configured form ID
submission_id: vendor submission ID
scan_id: random session identifier
submitted_at: ISO UTC timestamp
quiz_version / rules_version / content_version: v1 / v1 / v1
answers: object with Q01 through Q12 answer enums
context: NONE; preference: NONE
map_requested: true; email: the validated request address
followup_consent: false; consent_text_version: free-followup-v1
source_code: direct
result: {areaSummaries, strengths, gaps, firstAction, routeReason, versions}
Omit email when map_requested=false. Never copy the vendor’s submission PDF or preview URLs; they may contain access tokens.

GET /api/result and email link exchange
Browser cookie access is valid for 24 hours. Email uses /map#token=SIGNED_TOKEN, where the token contains scan_id, purpose=map, and a 90-day expiry. Fragment handling posts the token to /api/map-session, which verifies it, sets a short signed result cookie, and immediately removes the fragment from browser history. Disable third-party scripts on private pages. Bearer links can be forwarded; explicitly warn users. No email or answers in the token. Revocation checks deleted_at and expiry server-side.

Other endpoints
POST /api/feedback: cookie-authorized, CSRF protected, enum-only; queue to intake.
POST /api/selected-action: cookie-authorized and CSRF protected; accept only approved action IDs; update selection through intake without changing the original route.
POST /api/email-preferences: signed purpose-limited token, explicit change; queue to intake.
POST /api/internal/render-email: Make bearer credential; returns approved subject/body/parameters for a known job template and map snapshot. Never sends email itself.
POST /api/stripe: raw-body official signature verification; queues verified order changes.
All private responses are no-store; unauthenticated access never reveals whether an email address exists.

26 Reliability and operator runbook
Recover without duplicate delivery

Submission handling
Tally expects a prompt webhook response and retries failures. Keep signature validation and normalization fast; acknowledge only after durable queue acceptance. Alert the operator if the queue fails. Make incomplete executions must be retained with data loss disabled. A successful Tally response does not prove the email arrived; each state is tracked separately.

Single-writer rule
All record creation and state-change requests go through one sequential intake scenario. The email worker is the only component allowed to claim/send EmailJobs; it updates send state for existing jobs. Do not allow overlap between scheduled worker runs. Use Make’s scenario execution controls and verify with a long-running test. If those controls cannot guarantee one worker, do not launch automated sends until a durable lock is added.

Retry policy
For a definitive pre-send temporary failure, retry after 5 minutes, 30 minutes, and 2 hours, then mark failed for operator review. A timeout after submitting to Brevo is uncertain, because the provider may have accepted the email. Mark uncertain, inspect Brevo logs for the recipient/time/job correlation, and reconcile before any resend. Never assume a long-lived provider idempotency guarantee. Provider acceptance followed by a failed Airtable update is also uncertain.

Daily pilot check
Review uncertain sends, failed jobs, submissions with no result, and paid orders with no accepted delivery. Compare yesterday’s vendor submission count with stored scans. Reconcile Stripe paid sessions with Orders. Check suppression events. Use IDs and counts in alerts, not answer details or email contents. Target an operational acknowledgment within one business day; publish only a support promise the founder can maintain.

Common repairs
No result: check Tally delivery, Vercel error code, Make queue, then Airtable record. Never tell the user to submit again before checking for a saved record.
No email: inspect job, suppression state, sender authentication, and provider outcome. Correct a typo only after verifying the requester controls the new address. Resend only the specific requested message.
Wrong result: pause affected content version, fix the pure rule/adapter, run fixtures, and assess whether previous recipients need a factual correction. Do not silently re-score every map.

Pause and rollback
Disable new scans at the landing page if results cannot be produced. Hide checkout if fulfillment is broken. Pause promotional workers independently. Preserve IDs needed for recovery. Roll back to the last reviewed content and tested deployment; never substitute generated advice to keep the funnel running.

29 Validation metrics and pilot decisions
Measure useful action before software ambition

Event vocabulary
landing_view; scan_start; scan_submitted; result_viewed; map_requested; email_accepted; email_delivered; action_done; action_not_fit; offer_viewed; checkout_clicked; purchase_paid; refund. No raw answers, transition values, email addresses, or private map tokens in analytics. Use coarse source_code and version. Count action_done only from an explicit user choice.

Core definitions
Scan completion = unique valid submissions / unique scan starts.
Result visibility = scans with a viewed result within 24 hours / valid submissions.
Email request rate = scans requesting a map / valid submissions.
Activation = scans with at least one action_done within 7 days / scans whose result was viewed at least 7 days ago.
Action mismatch = scans with action_not_fit / result viewers; inspect by action ID with small-group privacy limits.
Purchase conversion = unique paid purchasers / unique eligible offer viewers, with a defined 14-day window.
Refund rate = refunded orders / paid orders old enough to complete the refund window.
Contribution per sale = price less fees, refunds, allocated delivery costs, and support time valued consistently.

Initial decision thresholds
These are hypotheses, not industry benchmarks. After 50 completed scans, seek at least 60% scan completion, 95% result availability within one minute under normal conditions, and 20% self-reported action completion within seven days. Missing follow-up feedback is not evidence that an action was completed. Aim for fewer than 10% “doesn’t fit” responses, but investigate any concerning comment or complaint regardless of count.

Paid pilot
After 100 eligible offer views, use five purchases as an initial willingness-to-pay signal, then seek ten paid customers before interpreting delivery and refund patterns. A small sample does not establish product-market fit. If purchases are low but free actions are used, revise the offer or audience before building more features. If actions are not used, improve the core product before selling more.

Weekly review
A single 30-minute review: funnel by source, action fit, delivery failures, paid fulfillment, refunds, and support minutes. Use counts beside percentages. Exclude controlled tests, known bots, and duplicates consistently. Do not optimize quiz wording to make people feel less prepared merely to increase sales.

Run a few bounded experiments

Experiment 1  One useful search page
Publish a practical page titled “How to make a simple backup plan for everyday responsibilities” with a genuinely usable blank checklist and one scan CTA. Use the same reviewed boundaries. Track source_code=search_checklist. Time budget: one initial writing session and monthly review. Experiment 2  Two aligned newsletters
Ask two small community or women-focused newsletters whether a free scan and practical checklist would serve their readers. Budget: two outreach sessions plus one follow-up, performed only when the founder authorizes sending. Compare result views and action completion, not just clicks.

Experiment 3  One community resource listing
With a moderator’s permission, share a useful free worksheet in one relevant community or resource directory. Make the paid offer transparent but secondary. Do not mass-post or imply endorsement. Track source_code=community_pilot and review after 20 valid scan completions or four weeks.

Operating limits
No daily social schedule, founder video requirement, large affiliate program, broad paid-ad spend, or automatic outreach in V0. These are proposed experiments, not campaigns already launched or claims about current market performance. Start with one or two channels and keep a simple weekly source table.

Upgrade to an app only when evidence requires it
Consider accounts and persistent plans when at least 20 paying customers have completed a journey, repeat usage is observable, and users repeatedly ask to update or revisit their plan. Consider a database/queue upgrade earlier if serialized automation becomes unreliable or expensive. Require clear economics and an owner for ongoing maintenance.

Possible next steps in order
First improve content and retention. Then replace Airtable/Make bottlenecks with a transactional database and reliable job queue. Only then consider optional accounts, saved progress, review reminders, and additional reviewed journeys. Sensitive document storage, contact networks, emergency services, and AI advice require a separate product, privacy, and safety design; they are not automatic upgrades.
