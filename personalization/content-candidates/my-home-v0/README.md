## The question

**What would help you feel more confident at home?**

Choose what matters to you. We'll start with three useful ideas.

- Keeping my home and personal details private
- Feeling comfortable with deliveries and home visits
- Knowing where to get help when something breaks
- Being ready for lockouts, outages and sudden problems
- Understanding my home and catching problems early

Multi-select. Optional text: **Anything specific you'd like help with?**

Example: “I rent, don't have anyone nearby, and want to know what I can do if maintenance doesn't respond.”

Helper: “We'll use what you share to tailor ideas for your home. Please leave out your address, door codes and other private access details.”

No new country or mandatory renter/owner screen. Known renting/owning context influences wording; otherwise conditional wording or one consequential clarification is enough. A checkbox expresses interest, not evidence that she lacks something. If she selects no boxes but submits a supported specific need, that need is enough; if both are empty, invite her to choose or type instead of pretending to personalize.

## How the choices affect suggestions

| Choice | Prioritize | Do not assume |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Private home/details | Address-removal options, location sharing, device access, window privacy | That her address is exposed, she is stalked, owns a camera or wants surveillance |
| Deliveries/visits | Parcel alternatives, permission/entry knowledge, visit confirmation and boundaries | That every visitor is threatening, she has a willing companion or can leave home for pickup |
| Help when something breaks | Responsibility, after-hours routes, consumer knowledge, existing support and suitable professionals | That she owns, must pay personally, has a current fault or should seek quotes for an imaginary job |
| Sudden problems | Authorized lockout routes, lighting/charging, water, official alerts and power dependencies | That every region has the same weather, building, services or accessible transport |
| Understanding/prevention | Alarm distinctions, visible early signs, recall checks, maintenance that actually applies and useful records | That a long cleaning schedule or more equipment is necessary |

## Research and writing method

The draft follows [discovery-writer-core.md](../../../docs/prompts/discovery-writer-core.md). Each card includes title, estimated active time, short context, exactly three practical steps and Done when. **26/56 tasks (46.4%) are dedicated learning tasks**, each with one supplemental copyable Google/AI search prompt. They contribute a useful distinction before asking her to research further. The other 30 prepare something tangible or usable. Time estimates are editorial active-effort estimates, not measured completion times; shopping, replies, appointments and service availability can add elapsed time.

Problem inspiration comes from the supplied Alone Actually reference, saved Buildpad research and directly opened Reddit discussions about home privacy, repair visits, lockouts and outages. Those discussions are qualitative leads, not prevalence, demographic verification or proof that a popular workaround is safe. During the first content pass, reliable vote counts were not exposed. The demand pass adds some indexed counts with access/date limits; none establishes task popularity or a “top-voted solution.” Official agency, regulator, manufacturer and provider pages check relevant solution facts. Scope and limitations are kept with each source. Broader study/survey evidence for choosing the category is already in the saved research; this package does not claim to establish a new market ranking.

Steps, titles, effort estimates, completion conditions, task groupings and priority assignments are our editorial work. A source supports a bounded fact or opportunity, not the efficacy of the complete task. Research-backed does not mean clinically proven or professionally certified. The collection is intentionally authored against the prompt; it was **not generated by a separate OpenAI API call**, and no paid model test is claimed.

## Resource and privacy rules

There are 26 resource-bearing cards and 30 self-contained cards. `source_ids` are internal evidence, not links automatically shown to users. Only `resource_ids` represent proposed in-card resources. The review displays `reader_title` and `reader_description` for these links; research provenance and editorial notes stay outside the task body.

Keep at most **one resource-bearing task in a three-card batch**, with at most two useful links or named services. Do not silently remove a task's essential resource just to meet the cap; choose another complementary task instead. A self-contained learning card can still have its copyable search prompt. Named services in text count toward this rule; the ledger isn't permission to recommend a local-only service nationally. Berkeley's tool library and Con Edison's reporting channel demonstrate possible routes, not universal user recommendations.

Do not collect or send home addresses, access codes, key locations, occupancy routines, documents, device credentials or account-recovery information to the AI. A city/state placeholder is only used when a local rule or resource matters. Exact model identifiers are acceptable only when non-sensitive and relevant. Personal records belong in the user's chosen private storage, not GitHub or the shared task pool.

## Boundaries and duplication review

- This is advance home preparation and learning. Active fire, gas odor, CO alarms, major flooding, immediate threats or unsafe exposure need the appropriate urgent route, not three preventive tasks. No diagnosis, lock bypass, hazardous DIY, weapon advice, fear tactics or promises of safety.
- Local law, rental agreements, manufacturer instructions, qualifications and actual policy terms control. No universal entry notice, deposit cap, rent-withholding instruction, guaranteed grant, coverage claim or assumed service availability.
- Privacy changes can be noticed. Active coercion or stalking needs a separate specialist-support route; do not automatically revoke access, confront someone or run a standard checklist.
- Do not force relationship-building, a test appointment or a purchase. A real device test that reveals an actual readiness gap is different from rehearsing a social interaction for engagement.
- Preserve outcome-level deduplication across categories. Delivery handling while away, phone power, practical home help and property records can overlap future categories or existing saved work. Reuse a canonical outcome when it is the same preparation; do not award it again under a new title.
- Potentially adjacent pairs: HM01/HM02 (data listing versus search visibility), HM05/HM13 (device viewing versus physical entry), HM17/HM21 (responsibility versus service type), HM23/HM24 (understanding an estimate versus agreeing a real change), HM34/HM35 (a usable lockout route versus hiring knowledge), HM41/HM42 (smoke versus CO). Preserve the distinction and avoid showing both merely to fill a batch.
